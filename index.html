Coverages

Six ways to cover a life

The bar under each card is the same lifetime you saw above — showing where that policy actually pays. Most households end up using two of these together.

Permanent Insurance

Coverage that stays in force for your whole life rather than expiring on a date. Premiums are higher than term, but the payout is guaranteed to arrive eventually — which is what makes it useful for estate and tax planning.

Runs for life

Whole Life Insurance

A permanent policy with a level premium that never rises and a cash value that grows on a guaranteed schedule. You can borrow against that value later, which is why it often doubles as a long-horizon savings vehicle.

Runs for life · accrues value

Term Insurance

The most coverage per dollar, for a fixed window — usually 10, 20 or 30 years. Built to cover the years you'd leave the biggest hole: a mortgage, dependent children, or an income the household still relies on.

A fixed window

Accident & Sickness Insurance

Replaces part of your income while an injury or illness keeps you off work, then stops once you're back. It covers the interruptions life insurance never touches — the months you survive but can't earn.

Only while you're off work

Critical Illness Insurance

One tax-free lump sum on diagnosis of a covered condition — commonly cancer, heart attack or stroke. There are no rules on how you spend it: treatment abroad, a mortgage payout, or replacing a partner's income while they care for you.

Pays once, on diagnosis

Funeral Insurance

A smaller policy sized to final expenses — the service, burial or cremation, and the bills that arrive with them. It pays quickly and usually accepts applicants that larger policies decline.

Pays at the end
